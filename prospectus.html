About the School

A small school with large intentions

GIGL International School was started with one belief — that a child in Darbhanga deserves the same quality of teaching, technology and care as a child in any metro city.

We keep our classes small, our classrooms digital, and our doors open. Parents are partners, not visitors. Every teacher here knows every child by name — their strengths, their worries, and the joke that makes them laugh.

Experienced Faculty

Qualified, trained teachers who enjoy teaching young children.

Activity Based Learning

Concepts are seen, touched and built — not memorised.

Digital Classrooms

Projector and audio in every room, used with purpose.

Safe & Nurturing Environment

A campus designed at a child's height.

Vision & Mission

What we are working towards

Our Vision

To be Darbhanga's most trusted school for early and primary education — the school parents recommend to other parents without being asked.

Our Mission

To teach with clarity, warmth and honesty; to keep learning joyful every day; and to send every child home a little more confident than they arrived.

How we measure ourselves. Not by toppers alone. We watch four things every term: attendance, reading levels, how confidently a child speaks up in class, and how they treat each other on the playground.

Holistic development is not a poster on our wall. It is a timetable decision — academics, arts, sport and values are all given real hours.

Page 6 · Leadership

Academics

CBSE pattern, child-paced

The syllabus follows the CBSE pattern from Nursery to Class 6th, but the pace is set by the child — not the calendar. Teachers keep a simple record of where each child is, and stretch or support accordingly.

  • English as the medium of instruction, with strong Hindi support.
  • Continuous, descriptive assessment — no public ranking of small children.
  • Regular parent-teacher meetings with written feedback.
  • Homework that a child can do without a parent's degree.
Page 7 · Academics

Scholastic Plan · Stage 1

Early Years — Nursery, LKG, UKG

The first job of school is to make a child love coming to it. Our Early Years programme is play-led, song-filled and gentle — with real pre-literacy and numeracy underneath the fun.

  • Phonics, pre-writing patterns and number sense
  • Story, rhyme, art, clay and free play
  • Colour days, festivals and rhymes on stage
  • No formal exams — observation and progress notes only

Class size: deliberately small, with a helper-supported model so that tie-laces, tears and toilet runs all get handled calmly.

Page 8 · Early Years

Scholastic Plan · Stage 2

Foundation Years — Class 1 to 4

Reading fluency, number confidence and neat handwriting are the three mountains of these years. We climb them with the child, not in front of them.

  • English, Hindi, Mathematics, EVS
  • Computer and digital literacy from Class 1
  • Art, music, dance and physical education every week
  • Continuous assessment with term report cards
Page 9 · Foundation Years

Scholastic Plan · Stage 3

Growing Years — Class 5 & 6

These are the years a child's habits set. We make the work honest, the feedback specific and the expectations clear — so secondary school feels like a step, not a shock.

  • English, Hindi, Sanskrit, Mathematics
  • Science, Social Science and Computer Science
  • Project work, presentations and Olympiad preparation
  • Term assessments with detailed subject feedback

Study skills: planner use, note-making and revision technique are taught explicitly — not assumed.

Student Life

A day at GIGL

Assembly and a thought for the day, then focused academic periods in smart classrooms. After the break: activity, lab or club time. The last period is for revision, reading or games — so homework has already half-happened before the child reaches home.

Through the year: colour days and festivals, Independence Day and Republic Day programmes, parent-teacher meetings, olympiads, excursions and the annual sports meet.

“The child should not be able to decide which was the better part of the day — the learning or the laughing.”
